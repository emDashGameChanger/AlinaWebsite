# Pricing and safety profile of the top 10 TR hits

Date: 2026-07-30
Scope: same top 10 as `literature-check.md` — ergotamine, berotralstat,
nilotinib, lomitapide, midostaurin, saquinavir, naldemedine, rimegepant,
conivaptan, bisoctrizole. Same rationale as
`../full-1E92-PTR1-screen/pricing-safety-profile.md`: a strong docking
score and even a real literature precedent don't matter if the drug can't
actually be cheap/safe/simple, which is the entire premise for looking at
visceral leishmaniasis in the first place.

All prices are current US list/retail figures pulled via web search
(GoodRx, Drugs.com, manufacturer patient sites) — order-of-magnitude, not
exact; they fluctuate and vary by insurance/country. Every figure and
safety claim below has a source link.

## Summary table

| Drug | Current approved use | Approx. US price | Key safety concerns |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ergotamine | Migraine (as Ergomar brand, or Cafergot-style caffeine/ergotamine combo) | Combo generic: ~$112-408/20 tabs w/ coupon; pure Ergomar brand: ~$872-1,643/20 tabs | Peripheral ischemia/vasospasm risk, especially with CYP3A4 inhibitors; contraindicated in cardiovascular disease and pregnancy |
| Berotralstat (Orladeyo) | Hereditary angioedema prophylaxis | ~$23,800/28-day supply (~$310,000/year) | QTc prolongation if more than 1 dose/day taken |
| Nilotinib (Tasigna) | Ph+ chronic myeloid leukemia | ~$5,450-15,000/month | **Black box**: QT prolongation, sudden death reported; also pancreatitis, liver problems, arterial occlusion |
| Lomitapide (Juxtapid) | Homozygous familial hypercholesterolemia | ~$54,400/28 capsules (~$200,000-300,000/year) | **Black box**: hepatotoxicity, hepatic steatosis; REMS-restricted distribution |
| Midostaurin (Rydapt) | FLT3-mutated AML, systemic mastocytosis | ~$13,100/28-day supply | Interstitial lung disease/pneumonitis warning; nausea, vomiting common |
| Saquinavir (Invirase) | HIV-1 infection (protease inhibitor, requires ritonavir boosting) | ~$344+/bottle, no generic available | New-onset/worsening diabetes, elevated blood sugar/lipids with ritonavir co-administration |
| Naldemedine (Symproic) | Opioid-induced constipation | ~$455-580/30 tabs/month | GI perforation risk; can precipitate opioid withdrawal symptoms |
| Rimegepant (Nurtec ODT) | Migraine (acute + preventive) | ~$900-1,100/month without insurance | Raynaud's phenomenon (new or worsening); hypersensitivity reactions |
| Conivaptan (Vaprisol) | Hyponatremia (IV, inpatient only) | ~$450-900/day (see PTR1 profile) | Contraindicated in hypovolemic hyponatremia/heart failure; 50+ drug interactions |
| Bisoctrizole | Sunscreen UV filter (topical only) | N/A — not an FDA-approved systemic/oral drug in the US | Not designed or tested for systemic/oral use; minimal skin absorption reported, but no data exists on what happens if taken orally |

## Bottom line

Same pattern as PTR1's top 10, with one real difference: **ergotamine, in
its common caffeine-combination generic form, is genuinely affordable**
(~$112-400 for 20 tablets w/ coupon) — a meaningfully better cost profile
than dihydroergotamine's per-dose pricing was for PTR1, and it's the top
hit on *two independent TR runs* rather than a single result. The pure
Ergomar brand formulation is expensive, so the specific formulation matters
a lot here, but a cheap generic version of the actual top-scoring molecule
existing at all is new for this project.

Everything else on the list falls into the same buckets seen before:
oncology/orphan drugs at $5,000-300,000+/year (nilotinib, lomitapide,
midostaurin, berotralstat) with black-box warnings on three of the four;
moderate-cost but non-generic drugs (saquinavir, naldemedine, rimegepant)
in the few-hundred-to-thousand-dollar/month range; conivaptan, already
ruled impractical for PTR1; and bisoctrizole, which isn't a real
repurposing candidate at all — it's a topical sunscreen ingredient with no
US approval or safety data for oral/systemic use, so its docking score is
academically interesting at most.

**Ergotamine is the clearest practical lead this project has found so
far** — cheap generic available, decades of real-world safety data (if a
serious, well-characterized vasospasm/ischemia risk profile, mainly via
drug interactions), and now a docking signal that's held up across two
different parasite targets and three independent runs. It's also,
notably, the *only* candidate on either target's combined top-10 lists
that clears the cost bar cleanly.

## Scope note

Per project-wide scope rules: none of this is a treatment recommendation.
Pricing and safety data describe these drugs' *current, approved* uses —
none of it says anything about what dose, formulation, or safety profile
they would have if ever actually tested against *Leishmania* or TR.
