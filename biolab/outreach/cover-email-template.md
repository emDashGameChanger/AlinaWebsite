# Cover email template — wet-lab collaboration outreach

Built with Claude Code. Pairs with the hosted one-pager at
`molecularDocking/ResearchOutreach.html` (intentionally unlinked from site
nav — direct-URL only), which this email links to. Use the to-do checklist
(`outreach-todo.html`, kept outside this repo) to verify everything below
before sending anything.

## Subject line options

- Repurposing candidates for visceral leishmaniasis — computational leads looking for a lab
- Student docking project, six candidates, no wet-lab access — worth a look?

## Template

> Hi Dr. [LAST NAME],
>
> I'm Alina Ren, a high school student working on a computational drug-repurposing
> project for visceral leishmaniasis — I came across your work on [SPECIFIC PAPER/TOPIC]
> and thought this might be relevant to what you're working on.
>
> Using AutoDock Vina-GPU and fpocket, I screened about 1,800 FDA-approved drugs against
> three *Leishmania* targets (PTR1, trypanothione reductase, and CYP51) and found six
> candidates that combine a strong docking score with either independent biological
> evidence or a cross-target specificity signal suggesting the hit is real rather than an
> artifact of the scoring function. [ONE SENTENCE ON WHY THIS RECIPIENT SPECIFICALLY —
> e.g. "digitoxin and irinotecan both show specific affinity for CYP51 in my screen, and
> I saw your group has worked on sterol pathway inhibitors in trypanosomatids."]
>
> I don't have wet-lab access, so I can't take this any further myself — the full
> write-up, methodology, and all raw data are here:
> [https://biomedbound.com/molecularDocking/ResearchOutreach.html]
>
> Would any of this be worth testing in an enzyme-inhibition or parasite growth-inhibition
> assay? Happy to share anything else that would help you evaluate it, and equally happy
> to hear if it's not a fit.
>
> Thanks for your time,
> Alina Ren
> alinaren@biomedbound.com

## Notes

- The bracketed "why this recipient" sentence is the part that actually matters —
  a generic mass email reads as spam and will be ignored; a one-line reason tied to
  their actual published work is what gets a response.
- DNDi's version should drop the "[SPECIFIC PAPER/TOPIC]" personalization and instead
  reference their public research-partnership/early-lead intake process directly — check
  their site for the current contact route before sending (see to-do list).
- Keep it this short. A long email is a request for someone's time before they've
  decided to give any; the one-pager is where the depth lives.
