Looking for a Wet-Lab Collaborator

Computational drug-repurposing candidates for visceral leishmaniasis, from a high schooler's docking pipeline — a research summary for anyone who could take these hits to an actual assay.

I'm Alina Ren, a high school student who wants to be a doctor. This page is a standalone summary written for researchers, not part of the site's regular tutorial flow — if you landed here from an email, that's exactly where I meant to send you. It lays out what I found, how confident I am in each piece, and what I'm actually asking for.

The short version

I screened ~1,800 FDA-approved drugs (AutoDock Vina-GPU + fpocket) against three Leishmania drug targets — pteridine reductase 1 (PTR1), trypanothione reductase (TR), and sterol 14α-demethylase (CYP51) — looking for repurposing candidates for visceral leishmaniasis. Six hits below combine a strong docking score with either independent biological evidence or a specificity signal suggesting the interaction is real rather than an artifact of Vina's scoring function. None of this is proof of efficacy. It's a prioritized, reproducible set of hypotheses that I don't have the means to test further myself — no wet lab, no cell culture, no assay access. If any of this is worth a day in an actual lab, I'd like to talk.

Method, briefly

  • Receptors: PTR1 (PDB 1E92, L. major, NADP+ cofactor retained), TR (PDB 2YAU, full A+B homodimer — its main pocket is inter-subunit), CYP51 (PDB 3L4D, chain A, heme retained).
  • Library: 1,795–1,859 FDA-approved drugs (DrugCentral), docked with AutoDock-Vina-GPU-2.1.
  • Pocket selection cross-checked against literature-reported binding-site residues where available (PTR1: matched 2 of 6 published key residues directly, the other 4 in adjacent fPocket fragments).
  • Every top-10 hit per target got a rendered pose check against the known pocket, not just a raw score.
  • Cross-target specificity analysis: since the same 1,795 drugs were screened against all three unrelated pockets, I z-scored each drug's affinity per target and computed a "specificity gap" — how much better a drug scores on its best target versus its own baseline on the other two. This separates drugs Vina genuinely favors for one pocket's shape from drugs it just scores well everywhere (a real confound with big, flexible, generically "sticky" molecules).

Six candidates worth a closer look

Candidate Target Docking score Independent evidence Practical notes
Digitoxin CYP51 −13.0 kcal/mol Digitoxigenin (its aglycone) confirmed IC50 = 6.9 µg/mL against L. infantum itself, selectivity index 42.8; β-acetyl-digitoxin reduced parasite load in vivo in mice. Specificity gap 2.47 (pocket-specific, not a generic sticky binder). Not marketed in the US (no longer manufactured there) or Canada; in Europe, currently only Germany, in short supply. No active registration found in Brazil (ANVISA) or India (CDSCO); nothing found in Bangladesh, Sudan, Ethiopia or Kenya either. Sold to researchers as a research chemical.
Irinotecan CYP51 −12.5 kcal/mol Confirmed active against L. infantum promastigotes and infected splenocytes via topoisomerase IB poisoning — a different mechanism than CYP51, so this corroborates the drug generally, not the docking hit specifically. Specificity gap 1.65. Cheap per vial (~$11/2 mL), but IV chemotherapy — requires infusion-center administration, real hematologic/GI toxicity.
Alectinib PTR1 −12.1 kcal/mol No direct Leishmania precedent found. Specificity gap 1.47 — genuinely pocket-specific, not just a strong binder everywhere. Expensive oncology drug: ~$12,500–19,000/month brand (~$70/month via patient assistance).
Bexarotene PTR1 −11.4 kcal/mol No direct precedent found. Specificity gap 1.28, and the single best ligand efficiency in the entire 1,795-compound dataset (0.438, from only 26 heavy atoms) — a small molecule fitting the pocket precisely rather than a big one racking up contacts. Retinoid, teratogenic; ~$9,469/month average retail (as low as ~$168 with a coupon).
Saquinavir TR −9.9 kcal/mol Confirmed active against L. major (IC50 7.0 µM) and, more weakly, L. infantum, via proteasome inhibition (Savoia, Allice & Tovo, Int J Antimicrob Agents, 2005) — a different mechanism than TR, so real but not a confirmation of this specific hit. ~$344+/bottle, no generic available.
Dutasteride CYP51 −12.6 kcal/mol No direct precedent found, but mechanistically the most plausible novel hit on this list — it's itself a steroid-pathway enzyme inhibitor, chemically close to CYP51's substrate class. Cheap generic, ~$8–40/month, no black-box warning — the best cost/safety profile the whole project has produced.

Full docking results, pose renders, literature checks, and pricing/safety sourcing for all three screens (not just these six) are available on request.

What I'm asking

I'm looking for a lab that can take any of these candidates one step further than a docking score — a recombinant PTR1/TR/CYP51 enzyme-inhibition assay, or a Leishmania promastigote/amastigote growth-inhibition assay, would both be a real answer to a question I can't currently answer myself. Everything I have is reproducible: receptor prep files, docking configs, full result sets, and the specificity analysis are all recorded, and I'm happy to share any of it, or anything else that would help someone evaluate whether this is worth their bench time.

Scope note

This is computational hypothesis-generation, not a therapeutic or clinical claim. Docking scores and cross-target specificity are evidence a drug's interaction with a pocket is physically plausible and probably not a scoring-function artifact — they are not evidence of biological activity, and none of the pricing or safety information above describes anything about how these drugs would behave if actually tested against Leishmania.
